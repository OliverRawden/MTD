Who needs MTD for Income Tax, and when

You need it if you're a sole trader or landlord registered for Self Assessment, with income from self-employment or property, and your qualifying income is over the threshold (GOV.UK):

Qualifying income overIn tax yearUse MTD from
£50,0002024 to 20256 April 2026 (already started)
£30,0002025 to 20266 April 2027
£20,0002026 to 20276 April 2028

Partnerships will join later; HMRC hasn't set a date. Some people can get an exemption, for example if digitally excluded. Source checked 29 October 2026.

Records software or bridging software?

GOV.UK describes two types: software that creates digital records (bank feeds, receipt scanning or manual entry) and usually does everything, and bridging software that connects existing records such as spreadsheets to HMRC (GOV.UK). You can use more than one product, but only one per submission.

Frequently asked questions

When do I need to use Making Tax Digital for Income Tax?

It depends on your qualifying income from self-employment and property. GOV.UK: over £50,000 in 2024 to 2025 means from 6 April 2026; over £30,000 in 2025 to 2026 means from 6 April 2027; over £20,000 in 2026 to 2027 means from 6 April 2028. Use HMRC's checker on GOV.UK to confirm.

Is there free MTD for Income Tax software?

Yes. 18 of the 103 products HMRC currently recognises have a free version, though HMRC notes free products may have limits, such as a limited number of transactions. See free MTD software.

Can I keep using spreadsheets?

Yes, with bridging software that connects your spreadsheet records to HMRC. 68 recognised products offer bridging. See bridging software.

When are quarterly updates due?

For both standard and calendar update periods the deadlines are 7 August, 7 November, 7 February and 7 May (GOV.UK). The tax return is still due by 31 January after the tax year.

Which MTD software does HMRC recommend?

None. HMRC says all listed software has passed its recognition process but it does not recommend any product or provider. This site doesn't either; it lists the facts so you can compare.
